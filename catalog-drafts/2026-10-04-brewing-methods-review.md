# Tea brewing methods: proposal for review

Researched 2026-10-04 against the current 23 tea recipes. Approved by the owner; implemented in release 2026-10-04.4-tea-methods.

## Recommendation

Treat a brewing method as a combination of style, vessel, ratio, temperature, timing and expected infusion range. Use three descriptive styles: Japanese short infusions, gongfu, and western pot. A glass vessel does not inherently dictate western brewing; that is the convenient default proposed for this owner's 500 mL pot.

There is no dependable universal multiplier that converts one style into another. Select a new reference recipe for the style, then scale leaf mass with water *within that recipe*. Do not double steep time automatically when water doubles. Repeated infusions reuse the original leaves; their count never multiplies the starting dose. Different styles need not produce identical flavor or identical lifetime liquid yield.

## Evidence and its limits

- [Hibiki-an's compact brewing guide](https://www.hibiki-an.com/getting_started.php/cnID/33) supports the existing 7.5 g / 200 mL reference: its range is 7–8 g. It distinguishes short houjicha and fukamushi steeps from longer gyokuro brewing. Its broader guide rounds the dose to 8 g; this small difference does not justify increasing the current kyusu dose.
- [Hibiki-an's general guide](https://www.hibiki-an.com/contents.php/cnID/3) supports reusing leaves, shorter second infusions, and adjusting temperature with time. This is why “always lengthen every successive steep” is unsuitable for Japanese greens.
- [Den's Tea brewing instructions](https://denstea.com/pages/brewing-instructions) show substantially different legitimate starting recipes: commonly 2–3 g per 4 US fl oz (about 118 mL), with short second infusions. This supports flexibility; it does not directly establish the western recipes below.
- [TeaVivre's Silver Needle](https://www.teavivre.com/organic-silver-needle-white-tea.html) provides a useful direct comparison: 2 g / 250 mL for 3–5 minutes in a cup, versus 5 g / 110 mL and nine increasingly long gongfu steeps. It is a different origin from the owner's Jinggu tea, so its schedule is an analogy rather than bag-specific authority.
- [Yunnan Sourcing's Sweet Ya Bao Q&A](https://yunnansourcing.com/products/wild-tree-purple-sweet-ya-bao-white-tea) recommends 5 g / 100 mL, 85–90°C and short gongfu infusions. Retain this tea's relatively generous dose instead of forcing every white tea onto one ratio.
- [Yunnan Craft's brewing discussion](https://www.yunnancraft.com/en/tea-brewing-tips) emphasizes differences in leaf form, processing, vessel and extraction. It supports starting conservatively and adjusting individual parameters, rather than treating every tea as interchangeable.

All newly proposed numerical western recipes and infusion ranges below are house estimates synthesized from this evidence, not vendor-tested conversions. The existing white-tea recipes remain starting points, not evidence that a particular bag reliably produces a stated number of cups. Count ranges include the first infusion; a rinse does not count. Stop when the flavor no longer appeals.

Second-infusions initially use the same temperature. These particular second-steep values need tasting more than the first-infusion references; the already-wetted Japanese leaves need not receive a longer second steep. Later matcha-iri cups should not be expected to reproduce the first cup's powder-rich appearance and texture.

Rationale: genmaicha gets the moderate 8 g / 500 mL trial previously discussed; sencha uses slightly cooler water for a longer infusion; fukamushi keeps a shorter infusion rather than being assigned the same two minutes; houjicha remains hot and relatively brief; gyokuro stays cool and slightly more concentrated. Call gyokuro's glass recipe “light pot style” to distinguish it from an especially concentrated umami-focused preparation. A fine strainer and complete draining matter, particularly for fukamushi.

Within the new genmaicha glass method, 250 mL would use 4.0 g and two minutes; 500 mL would use 8.0 g and two minutes. This is intentional proportional scaling inside a different brewing style. The kyusu retains 9.4 g / 250 mL and one minute, with more of the session spread over short infusions.

## Proposed app behavior

1. Label the existing choices with style: “Kyusu · Japanese”, “Glass · western”, “Gaiwan · gongfu”, and “Zisha · gongfu”. Style describes the selected recipe, not an inherent rule about the vessel material.
2. Store expected infusion range and guidance on each method. Display the selected method's guidance beside its water recommendation. A shared product-level override must not conceal a method's different count.
3. Keep **planned steeps** separate from **typical potential**. Example: “Today: 2 × 250 mL = 500 mL. These leaves typically support 2–3 infusions.” Potential remains informational and does not force extra cups or multiply the leaf dose.
4. Preserve current volume choices: glass 200–500 mL for one selected pot, kyusu 250/500 mL sessions, gaiwan 100–500 mL, zisha in full 110 mL pours. Do not silently expand them to match an estimated maximum.
5. For the first update, put optional glass re-steep timing in the guidance. A separate “Brew another infusion” control could follow if desired; it would reuse the original leaves and show additional cumulative water without requiring a 1 L pot. This extra control is not required to correct the recipes.
6. If a selected session exceeds a method's typical range, show a light “Later infusions may be weak” note; do not impose a hard cutoff. Any extrapolated timer stays explicitly estimated.
7. Keep house water recommendations unchanged in this proposal so tasting the recipe change does not simultaneously change water. Future purchase research should provide method-specific recipes and counts, rather than clone one recipe into every suitable vessel.

## Suggested tasting check

Start with Genmaicha Matcha-iri: compare the existing kyusu session (9.4 g, two 250 mL infusions with its existing 60/45-second timings) with the proposed glass pot (8.0 g, 500 mL, two minutes), both at 80°C and with the same house water. They are different preparations, so assess enjoyment rather than expecting identical cups. Taste the glass second infusion separately before deciding whether “1–2” suits your preference. If the first cup is thin, try 10 g next time; if harsh, shorten the steep before increasing it further.
